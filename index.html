Why is human oversight necessary?
Case 1 of 4 · Military · "Lavender" AI Targeting System, Gaza
Israel's military reportedly used an AI system called Lavender to mark tens of thousands of Palestinians as targets, with officers spending as little as 20 seconds per name - often just confirming the target was male - before authorizing a strike.
+972 Magazine / Local Call investigation, 2024
Case 2 of 4 · Welfare & Tax · Dutch Childcare Benefits Scandal
A self-learning algorithm flagged parents as fraud risks, using dual nationality as a signal. Over 26,000 families were wrongly accused, plunged into debt, and in more than 1,600 cases had their children taken into foster care.
Dutch parliamentary inquiry report, 2020
Case 3 of 4 · Criminal Justice · COMPAS Recidivism Algorithm
A machine-learning algorithm scored defendants' risk of reoffending - nearly twice as likely to wrongly flag Black defendants as high-risk. Nobody had checked the model for that bias before judges started relying on it.
ProPublica, “Machine Bias,” 2016
Case 4 of 4 · Education · UK A-level Grading Algorithm, 2020
With exams cancelled in 2020, Ofqual used an algorithm to calculate final grades instead of trusting teachers. Nearly 40% of results were downgraded - hitting state school students hardest, with no real way to challenge a grade before it cost someone their place.
CNBC / Ofqual results data, 2020
The law · EU AI Act, Article 14
The EU AI Act now requires high-risk AI systems to be designed so they "can be effectively overseen by natural persons" - but leaves who watches, when they check, and what triggers a stop entirely up to whoever writes the policy.
Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 14, Human Oversight
The gap
Governments recognize human oversight is needed - they just don't specify how it works.

WHY THIS EXISTS

The gap between regulatory language and practical implementation

Most oversight definitions sound plausible in a document. They name values, invoke principles, and gesture toward accountability. But when an AI system behaves unexpectedly, definitions are stress-tested in ways their authors never imagined.

Signal Check asks four concrete questions of any definition: Who is the human in the loop? When do they look? How would they know something is wrong? And can they actually do anything about it?
